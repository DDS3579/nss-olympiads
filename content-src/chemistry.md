## PART 0B — How the four levels map onto Chemistry in Nepal

### 🎉 The good news: Chemistry is the subject where your four levels are **all real**

For Physics I had to tell you the district level doesn't exist. **For Chemistry it does.** Nepal's chemistry olympiad runs a genuine three-stage national ladder — which is exactly what MoEST's guideline contemplates — and it is the closest match to your District / Province / National / International model of any Nepali subject.

### 0B.1 The legal framework (same law as Physics)

**राष्ट्रियस्तरको STEM ओलम्पियाड सञ्चालनसम्बन्धी कार्यविधि, २०८२** (MoEST)

| Clause | Requirement | Chemistry's compliance |
|---|---|---|
| **दफा ३(४)** | ≥ 2 stages: **district *OR* province**, then national | ✅ Exceeds it — runs **district AND province AND national** |
| **दफा ३(६) / ८(२)** | Project-based **training / mentoring** between Stage 1 and Stage 2 | ✅ **"Chemistry Olympiad Experience Sharing and Learning Camp"** |

# 🟦 PART 1 — LEVEL 1: DISTRICT

### 1.1 Status of this level

✅ **This level genuinely exists** — STAN runs a district round and publishes a District Selection List.

❌ **But nothing about it is published.** There is no website, no syllabus, no sample paper, no past paper, no marking scheme, no registration portal outside Facebook. A student in a district town cannot find out what the district round tests without asking a teacher who happens to be a STAN member.

**What legitimately occupies this slot:** the **NEB Grade 11 Chemistry curriculum**. District rounds in every comparable country (USNCO Local, UKChO Round 1, BdChO Selection) test school-syllabus breadth at speed — not exotic topics.

# 🟥 PART 4 — LEVEL 4: INTERNATIONAL  *(IChO)*

### 4.1 The single most important strategic fact

The official IChO theory syllabus has **326 numbered entries**, each tagged **Level 1, 2 or 3**. Verified counts (extracted directly from the syllabus PDF):

| Level | Count | % | Definition (verbatim from the syllabus) | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Level 1** | **95** | 29 % | *"included in the overwhelming majority of secondary school chemistry programs and **need not to be mentioned in the preparatory problems**"* | = the NEB curriculum. You get this from school. |
| **Level 2** | **55** | 17 % | *"included in a substantial number of secondary school programs and **maybe used without exemplification** in the preparatory problems"* | ⚠️ Can appear **cold**. Partially in NEB. |
| **Level 3** | **176** | **54 %** | *"**not** included in the majority of secondary school programs and **can only be used in the competition if examples are given in the preparatory problems**"* | 🎯 **The preparatory problems ARE the syllabus.** More than half the IChO is unreachable except through them. |

> **What this means for Nepal, concretely:** over half of what IChO can test is *by regulation* signposted in advance, in the **preparatory problem set** that the host country must distribute to all participating countries **in January of the competition year** (IChO Regulations ¶10: *"not lower than 25 theoretical and 5 experimental tasks"*). Nepal receives this document every year. **Working that document systematically is the highest-return activity available to a Nepali chemistry olympiad student** — and nothing in Nepal currently organises it. → Gap **C-5**.

**Assumption:** a Grade-11 student starting in Kartik (November), targeting the NOoN District Round, then Provincial (early April), then NChO National (mid-April).

**Four non-negotiable habits:**
1. **Work the IChO preparatory problems, not random problems.** By regulation, Level-3 content (54 % of the syllabus) can *only* be examined if it appears there.
2. **Learn mechanisms, not reactions.** Klein → Clayden is the only reliable route through IChO §9.
3. **Do lab work every single week from week 25.** The IChO practical is 5 hours. Nepal's only medal was won in a remote IChO with no practical. Do not repeat that dependency.
4. **Practise in Hindi/Devanagari occasionally** using INChO's Hindi papers — it builds the bilingual vocabulary you'll want if you ever write in Nepali.
