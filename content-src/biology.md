## 📉 PART 0B — THE DIAGNOSTIC: exactly where Nepal loses (original analysis)

The IBO publishes **per-component raw scores**. I parsed the complete **IBO 2026 individual results** (302 competitors, 78 countries) and compared Nepal's 4 students against the **top 10 gold medallists**, component by component, using the IBO's own published column labels.

### IBO 2026, Vilnius — Nepal vs the gold-medal top 10

| Component (IBO's own labels) | Gold top-10 avg | **Nepal avg** | **Nepal as % of gold** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LAB 1 — Molecular Biology** | 88.55 | **14.00** | 🔴 **16 %** |
| **LAB 2 — Animal Physiology** | 82.03 | **19.19** | 🔴 **23 %** |
| **LAB 3 — Animal Morphology** | 71.44 | **28.55** | 🟠 **40 %** |
| **LAB 4 — Plant Comparative Biology** | 53.16 | **10.71** | 🔴 **20 %** |
| **PRACTICAL TOTAL (4 labs)** | **295.19** | **72.44** | 🔴🔴 **25 %** |
| **Theory A** | 44.34 | **23.35** | 🟡 **53 %** |
| **Theory B** | 42.26 | **22.20** | 🟡 **53 %** |
| **THEORY TOTAL** | **86.60** | **45.55** | 🟡 **53 %** |
| **FINAL SCORE** | **88.99** | **10.90** | 🔴 **12 %** |

### The four findings

**① Nepal's biology problem is a LABORATORY problem, not a knowledge problem.**
On theory, Nepal performs at **53 % of gold-medal level** — respectable for a first-decade programme. On the four practicals, Nepal performs at **25 %**. The practical deficit is **more than twice** the theory deficit. Since the IBO is **4 labs × 1.5 h = 6 practical hours** versus **2 theory papers × 3 h = 6 theory hours**, the practical is *half the exam* — and Nepal scores a quarter of gold there.

**② Molecular Biology (LAB 1) is the single worst component: 16 % of gold-medal performance.**
Nepal averaged **14.00 / ~100** where gold medallists averaged **88.55**. Plant Comparative Biology (LAB 4) is nearly as bad at **20 %**. These are precisely the two domains that require **bench techniques Nepal's schools do not teach** — gel electrophoresis, micropipetting, PCR, chromatography, centrifugation, staining, sectioning.

**③ Nepal is 2.16× away from a bronze — a measurable, closable gap.**

| | Value |
|---|---|
| IBO 2026 field | 302 competitors · 78 countries · **33 Gold, 60 Silver, 92 Bronze, 29 Merit** |
| **Bronze cut-off** | **rank 185, final score 38.56** (Jeslyn Jie Ting, Malaysia) |
| **Nepal's best** | Ahmon Gurung, **rank 288, final score 17.83** |
| **Gap to bronze** | **+20.73 points — Nepal must score 2.16× better** |
| Gold reference | Ziheng Wang (China), rank 1, final score 93.12 |

Nepal's best student reached **46 % of the bronze threshold.** Because the practical is where the deficit lives, and because practical technique is *trainable in months* rather than years, this is the most closable gap of any Nepali olympiad subject.

**④ Nepal's best IBO result of 2026 was 288th of 302 — and that student was Nepal's national champion.**
`Ronish Aryal` is publicly recorded as **"1st Rank & National Winner – National Biology Olympiad (NBO 2026)"**. At the IBO he placed **301st of 302** with a final score of **4.72**. Winning the NBO and finishing second-from-last at the IBO in the same year is the clearest possible measure of the distance between Nepal's national standard and the international one. → **Gap B-3.**

### 2.4 ⭐ The official IBO theoretical topic distribution — and why it matters more than any syllabus

**The IBO has no syllabus.** Its Operational Guidelines v.7 state plainly: *"the IBO currently has no syllabus on topics that should be studied or can be assessed."* Its participation page adds: *"There is no fixed syllabus or curriculum for the IBO… Many countries use university level general biology textbooks in their final preparation rounds."*

**But §3.7.3 of the Operational Guidelines does fix the topic proportions**, and these are binding on the exam setters:

> **(3) Theoretical questions should cover the following topics in the indicated proportions:**
> **I. Cell biology (20 %)** — structure and function of cells and their components; microbiology; biotechnology
> **II. Plant anatomy and physiology (15 %)** — *with emphasis on seed plants*
> **III. Animal anatomy and physiology (25 %)** — *with emphasis on vertebrates and especially man*
> **IV. Ethology (5 %)**
> **V. Genetics and Evolution (20 %)**
> **VI. Ecology (10 %)**
> **VII. Biosystematics (5 %)**
>
> **(1)** The IBO theoretical tasks should test the competitors' **understanding, processing skills and application** of biological knowledge.
> **(2)** **Questions should require understanding, rather than knowledge.**

📄 <https://www.ibo-info.org/files/downloads/rules/IBO%20Operational%20Guidelines_v.7.pdf> ✅ LIVE (507 KB, 22 pp)

> **Note the perfect alignment:** Bioly.guide's six handout libraries (S10–S15) map **one-to-one onto these seven domains** (ecology + ethology combined). Using them means studying in the exact proportions the exam is weighted. No other free resource does this.

### 3.2 ⭐ THE NATIONAL-LEVEL SYLLABUS MISMATCH (original analysis)

Nepal's own IBO filing says NBO study materials are *"High School Textbooks and reference books from University."* So the NEB curriculum is the operative syllabus. I mapped **all 256 official NEB Biology teaching hours** onto the **IBO's binding §3.7.3 domain weights**:

| IBO domain | **IBO exam weight** | NEB teaching hours | **NEB share** | **Gap** | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I. Cell biology** | **20 %** | 27 h | **12.1 %** | **−7.9 pp** | 🔴 **under-taught** |
| **II. Plant anatomy & physiology** | 15 % | 28 h | 12.5 % | −2.5 pp | 🟡 matched |
| **III. Animal anatomy & physiology** | **25 %** | 42 h | **18.8 %** | **−6.2 pp** | 🔴 **under-taught** |
| **IV. Ethology** | **5 %** | 3 h | **1.3 %** | **−3.7 pp** | 🔴 **3.7× under-weight** |
| **V. Genetics & Evolution** | **20 %** | 36 h | **16.1 %** | **−3.9 pp** | 🟠 under-taught |
| **VI. Ecology** | 10 % | 24 h | 10.7 % | +0.7 pp | ✅ matched |
| **VII. Biosystematics** | **5 %** | **64 h** | **28.6 %** | **+23.6 pp** | 🔴🔴 **5.7× OVER-weight** |

### The three conclusions

**① Nepal teaches the wrong distribution.** The NEB curriculum spends **28.6 % of its biology hours on biosystematics** — Floral Diversity (30 h) and Faunal Diversity (34 h), i.e. memorising fungal classes, algal groups, phyla and family descriptions. **The IBO weights that domain at 5 %.** That is a **5.7× over-investment** in the least-examined topic.

**② The IBO's two heaviest domains are under-taught.** Cell biology (20 %) + Animal anatomy & physiology (25 %) = **45 % of the IBO exam**. NEB devotes **30.8 %** of hours to them — a **14.2 percentage-point shortfall**.

**③ Ethology is effectively absent.** 3 hours out of 256, against a 5 % exam weight — and it is a *standalone* IBO domain and a *standalone* IBO practical (the IBO 2023 Practical 3 was "Ecology and ethology").

> **This is not a criticism of the NEB curriculum.** It is an excellent *school* syllabus — it is designed for the NEB board exam and for MECEE-BL medical entrance, where biodiversity and human physiology dominate. **The problem is that Nepal's olympiad selects from the school syllabus and then sends students to an exam weighted completely differently.** A student who is top of their NEB Biology class is, by construction, over-trained in 5 % of the IBO and under-trained in 45 % of it.

### 3.3 ⭐ THE PRACTICAL-SKILLS GAP (original analysis)

The IBO's **Appendix 2: List of practical skills** (Operational Guidelines v.7, pp. 17–20) lists seven sections of testable laboratory skills. I cross-referenced every one against the NEB Biology curriculum:

| IBO Appendix 2 section | Skills required | NEB coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Science Process skills** | Observation · Measurement · Grouping/classification · **Relationship finding** · Calculation · **Data organization & presentation (graphs, tables, charts, diagrams, photographs)** · **Prediction/projection** · **Hypothesis formulation** · **Operational definition (scope, condition, assumption)** · **Variable identification and control** · **Experimentation: design, execution, recording, interpretation, conclusion** · **Representing numerical results with appropriate accuracy** | ⚠️ Partial — NEB practical is 25 marks internal, school-assessed, and covers microscopy/slide prep/dissection observation/field visits/project reports. **Experimental design, hypothesis formulation, variable control and error/accuracy treatment are not taught.** |
| **II. Basic biological skills** | Magnifying-glass observation · **Microscope (objective max. 45×)** · **Stereomicroscope** · **Drawing of preparations** · Exact description of a biological drawing using coded term tables | ⚠️ Microscopy yes; **stereomicroscope no**; **scientific biological drawing to IBO standard no** |
| **III-A. Cytological methods** | **Maceration and squash technique** · **Smear method** · **Staining of cells and slide preparation** | ❌ **None** |
| **III-B. Plant anatomy & physiology methods** | **Dissection of plant flower and deduction of flower formula** · Dissection of roots/stems/leaves/fruits · **Free-hand sectioning** of stems, leaves, roots · **Staining (e.g. lignin)** and slide prep of plant tissues · **Elementary measurement of photosynthesis** · **Measurement of transpiration** | ⚠️ Flower dissection & flower formula **are** in NEB G11 U2 (taught as theory/description). **Free-hand sectioning, staining, photosynthesis measurement and transpiration measurement: none.** |
| **III-C. Animal anatomy & physiology methods** | **Dissection of invertebrates** · **Whole-mount slide preparation of small invertebrates** · **Elementary measurement of respiration** | ⚠️ Earthworm and frog *anatomy* are theory in NEB G11 U8. **Actual dissection, whole-mount preparation and respirometry: none.** |
| **III-D. Ethological methods** | **Determination and interpretation of animal behavior** | ❌ **None** (3 h of theory only) |
| **III-E. Ecological & environmental methods** | **Estimation of population density** · **Estimation of biomass** · **Elementary estimation of water quality** · **Elementary estimation of air quality** | ⚠️ Ecological field visits are listed in the NEB practical; **quantitative population-density/biomass estimation and water/air quality assays are not** |
| **III-F. Taxonomic methods** | **Use of dichotomous keys** · **Construction of simple dichotomous keys** · Identification of the most common flowering plant families · Identification of insect orders · Identification of phyla and classes | ⚠️ Family/phylum/class *identification by description* is heavily taught (64 h). **Dichotomous keys — use or construction — are not taught at all**, despite being the standard IBO taxonomic instrument |
| **IV. Physical & chemical methods** | **Chromatography · filtration · centrifugation** · **Standard tests for monosaccharides, polysaccharides, lipids, protein (Fehling, I₂ in KI, biuret)** · **Titration** · **Measuring quantities by drip and strip methods** · **Dilution methods** · **Pipetting incl. micropipettes** · **Microscopy incl. counting chambers** · **Determination of absorption of light** · **Gel electrophoresis** | ❌ **None.** Biochemical food tests appear in NEB G11 U1 as *theory* only. |
| **V. Microbiological methods** | **Preparing nutrient media** · **Aseptic techniques (flaming, heating glassware)** · **Inoculation techniques** | ❌ **None** (5 h of microbiology theory only) |
| **VI. Statistical methods** | **Probability and probability distributions** · **mean, median, percentage, variance, standard deviation, standard error, t-test, chi-square test** | ❌ **None in Biology.** Some statistics exist in NEB *Mathematics*, but never applied to biological data. |
| **VII. Bioinformatics methods** *(added after IBO 2026)* | **A.** Comparative sequence analysis — performing/interpreting alignments, identifying point mutations and indels, comparing nucleotide/amino-acid sequences · **B.** Phylogenetic analysis — interpreting trees, **distinguishing orthologous vs paralogous genes**, inferring evolutionary relationships · **C.** Querying biological databases — searching, interpreting records, **using accession numbers and cross-referencing** · **D.** Genomic/transcriptomic data — interpreting sequencing outputs, comparing gene expression, identifying trait-associated genes · **E.** Structural bioinformatics — inspecting protein structures, identifying secondary structure/hydrogen bonds/hydrophobic regions, comparing homologous structures · **F.** **Genetic engineering design (virtual cloning)** — identifying restriction enzyme sites, **designing plasmid constructs**, **designing PCR primers** · **G.** Modelling of biological systems — interpreting simulation outputs, parameter sensitivity · **H.** Integration of multiple biological data sources | ❌ **Zero coverage — the entire section.** NEB mentions "genetic engineering and GMOs" as 7 hours of descriptive theory. |

**Count: of the IBO's 7 practical-skills sections, NEB fully covers 0, partially covers 4, and does not cover 3 at all.**

> **This is the explanation for PART 0B.** Nepal scores **25 % of gold-medal level on the practicals** and **16 % on Molecular Biology (LAB 1)** because Nepali students have **never held a micropipette, never run a gel, never plated a culture, never performed a titration, never computed a standard error on their own data, and never aligned a sequence.** The IBO's own guidance says the exam tests *"practical skills, biological insight, analytical approach and capacity for problem solving"* — **not** *"knowledge reproduction (i.e. factual based questions, easily prepared by rote memorization)."* Nepal's preparation is almost entirely rote-factual.

**Four non-negotiable habits:**
1. **Study to the IBO weights, not the NEB weights.** Animal A&P + Cell biology = **45 % of the exam**. Biosystematics = **5 %**, even though NEB gives it 28.6 % of its hours.

4. **Practise in the IBO's item format.** The IBO uses **~100 largely closed-ended tasks, e.g. multiple true-false** — not NEB's MCQ/short/long structure. IBO 2024 Theory A and B are free; use them as your format template.
