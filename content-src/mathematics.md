## 📉 PART 0B — THE DIAGNOSTIC: where Nepal actually loses (original analysis)

The IMO scores each of its 6 problems out of 7. `imo-official.org` publishes **per-problem scores** for every contestant. I transcribed all **60 Nepali contestant-results (360 problem attempts)** from the official database and tabulated them. **This analysis does not exist anywhere else — not on MAN's site, not in any Nepali source.**

### Nepal's IMO scoring by problem position, 2017–2026

| Problem | Points won | Max | **Score rate** | Attempts scoring **0** | Zero rate | Perfect 7s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** (Day 1 easy) | **109** | 420 | **26.0 %** | 27 / 60 | 45.0 % | **6** |
| **P2** (Day 1 medium) | **8** | 420 | **1.9 %** | **53 / 60** | **88.3 %** | 0 |
| **P3** (Day 1 hard) | **1** | 420 | **0.2 %** | **59 / 60** | **98.3 %** | 0 |
| **P4** (Day 2 easy) | **83** | 420 | **19.8 %** | 41 / 60 | 68.3 % | **8** |
| **P5** (Day 2 medium) | **23** | 420 | **5.5 %** | 45 / 60 | 75.0 % | 0 |
| **P6** (Day 2 hard) | **4** | 420 | **1.0 %** | **59 / 60** | **98.3 %** | 0 |
| **TOTAL** | **228** | **2520** | **9.0 %** | | | |

### The four findings that should drive your entire programme

**① 84.2 % of every point Nepal has ever scored at the IMO came from Problems 1 and 4.**
P1 + P4 = 192 of 228 points. The other four problems contributed 36 points between them across ten years.

**② Problems 3 and 6 are effectively blank.** 59 of 60 attempts scored **zero** — a 98.3 % shut-out rate on each. In ten years, exactly **one** Nepali student has ever scored a single point on an IMO Problem 3 (Nishan Khatri, 2026), and exactly **one** on a Problem 6 (Suraj Acharya, 2021, 4 pts).

**③ Problem 2 is the highest-leverage target in Nepali mathematics.** P2 is the *medium* problem of Day 1 — harder than P1, far easier than P3 — yet Nepal scores **1.9 %** on it and **zeroes it 88 % of the time**. Closing P2 from 1.9 % to even 25 % would add ~98 points across a decade, roughly **quintupling** Nepal's P2 haul. This is a *technique* gap, not a talent gap: students who can solve P1 are being handed P2 and scoring 0.

**④ The trend is genuinely, steeply improving.** Total team points by year:

| 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 5 | 17 | 4 | 15 | 27 | 26 | 28 | 46 | **57** |

**3 → 57 points in ten years, a 19× improvement**, with 2026 the best result ever (3 HMs). The 2025 bronze was not a fluke; the programme is compounding. MAN was founded in **2015** and this curve starts in **2017**.

> **Strategic conclusion for NSS Olympiad Hub:** don't build a generic "olympiad maths" library. Build a **P2/P5 bridge programme** — material that takes a student who can reliably solve P1/P4 and teaches them the *middle-difficulty* techniques (invariants, careful case analysis, standard lemmas applied non-standardly). And build a **P3/P6 exposure programme** so students at least attempt them: 98.3 % zeros means most Nepali students never write anything on the hardest problem, forfeiting the partial credit (1–2 points) that is freely available for a correct definition, a lemma statement, or a special case.

### 🚨 1.3 CRITICAL: the DMO syllabus contradicts MAN's own website

This is the single most important practical finding in this document.

MAN's website prints the **same "Core Curriculum & Topic Areas" block verbatim on all five programme pages** (DMO, PMO, NMO, TST, IMO):

> **Algebra & Inequalities** — Polynomials, functional equations, AM-GM, Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities, sequences & recurrences.
> **Number Theory** — Divisibility, primes, modular arithmetic, Euler totient theorem, Diophantine equations, **LTE lemma**.
> **Euclidean Geometry** — Triangles, cyclic quadrilaterals, power of a point, radical axis, **projective geometry**, **homothety**.
> **Combinatorics & Graph Theory** — Counting principles, pigeonhole principle, invariant principles, graph coloring, extremal combinatorics.

**Compare that against the actual published DMO syllabus PDF:**

| Website claims DMO tests | Actually in the DMO syllabus PDF? |
|---|---|
| Functional equations | ❌ **absent** |
| AM-GM, Cauchy-Schwarz | ❌ only generic "Inequalities" |
| **Number theory: primes, modular arithmetic, Euler's totient, Diophantine equations, LTE lemma** | ❌ **entirely absent — the word "prime" does not appear** |
| Cyclic quadrilaterals, power of a point, radical axis, projective geometry, homothety | ❌ **entirely absent** — the PDF's geometry is *mensuration* (areas, surface areas, volumes) |
| Pigeonhole, invariants, graph colouring, extremal combinatorics | ❌ **absent** — the PDF's combinatorics is school-level permutation & combination |
| — | ✅ **but the PDF DOES require**: matrices, determinants & linear systems · complex numbers & **De Moivre's theorem** · vectors (dot & cross product) · VAT/discount/commission/tax · population growth & depreciation · surface areas & volumes of combined solids — **none of which appear in the website's "core curriculum"** |

**What this means for a student:** if you prepare for the DMO using the website's "Core Curriculum", you will study the Lifting-the-Exponent Lemma and radical axes — **and be tested instead on VAT calculations, matrices and De Moivre's theorem.** Conversely, the DMO syllabus PDF is essentially the **NEB Grade 9–12 school curriculum** (it maps almost exactly onto the official CDC Grade 11–12 Mathematics course: Algebra, Trigonometry, Analytic Geometry, Vectors, Statistics & Probability, Calculus).

**Resolution:** the DMO is a **school-mathematics speed-and-accuracy test**. The olympiad four-pillar syllabus on the website describes the **NMO/TST/IMO** level, and has been copy-pasted onto every page. Prepare for DMO from the **syllabus PDF + NEB curriculum**, and start the four-pillar olympiad work **only after you qualify for PMO**. → Gap **M-1**.
