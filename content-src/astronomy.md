## PART 0B — What makes Astronomy structurally different (and easier to break into)

Four features distinguish astronomy from the other six subjects on your site. Each is a strategic opportunity.

### ① There is **no district round** — the ladder starts at Province

Unlike Biology (Local → District → Province → National) and Mathematics (DMO → PMO → NMO → TST), the NAO has **three stages, beginning with the Provincial Round.** There is no district tier and no MoEST-मandated district/province choice — NASO goes straight to province. → **Gap A-1.**

### ② The **application form is scored** — unique in Nepal

Verified from the official NAO syllabus PDF and the NAO2025 Guidelines:

> *"Application file itself consist of **10 marks** (question in the application form = **5 marks** and SEE = **5 marks**)"*

The 5 marks for "the question in the application form" are for the **motivation statement**: *"Fill it carefully, including a short motivation statement (worth 5 marks)."* **No other Nepali olympiad scores the application.** For a student from a district school with a weaker SEE score, a well-written motivation statement is 5 free marks out of 100. → **Gap A-2.**

### ③ **No negative marking** — unlike Mathematics' DMO

> *"There will be **no negative marking**."*

So the optimal strategy at NAO Round 1 is to **attempt every question**, the opposite of the DMO. Students who also sit the Maths DMO carry the wrong instinct across. → **Gap A-3.**

### ④ The syllabus is **explicitly non-calculus**

From the official IOAA Syllabus, Appendix A.1 General Notes, clause 2 (verified from the IOAA Problem Book):

> *"Basic concepts in physics and mathematics at high school level are required in solving the problems. **Standard solutions should not involve use of calculus and/or the use of complex numbers and/or solving differential equations.**"*

Astronomy olympiad is therefore **the most accessible** of the science olympiads for a Nepali Grade 11–12 student: no calculus required, and the physics needed is a subset of the NEB Physics syllabus you have already documented. The barrier is **breadth of astronomical knowledge + observational skill + data analysis**, not mathematical sophistication.

### ⭐ The single most important strategic fact

**Astronomy has the deepest free training ecosystem of any subject in this series — and two of its best resources were written by Nepali students.**

|---|---|---|
| **"National Astronomy Olympiad (Nepal)"** — 7-page country advice document | ⭐ **Osish Niraula, IOAA 2024 & 2025 (Nepal)** | The **only** Nepal-specific olympiad strategy document published in an international venue. Hosted on aoguide.app alongside country guides for India, Romania, Singapore and Bangladesh. |
| **"Practical Handouts for Olympiad"** | ⭐ **Ayush Tiwari** — *a member of Nepal's own IOAA 2025 team* | *"Covers all aspects of data analysis and most observation skills required for IOAA."* Written by a serving Nepali team member. |

| **Basic Astronomy and Astrophysics: IOAA Training Book** | **Aniket Sule** — *Academic Coordinator of the Indian Astronomy Olympiad* | The recommended first book, named in Nepal's own advice document |

Nepal's astronomy programme punches above its weight **because its alumni publish.** That is the model NSS Olympiad Hub should replicate for the other six subjects.

### 1.1 Status of this level

❌ **There is no district round in Nepali astronomy.** NASO's three stages begin with the Provincial Round.

What legitimately occupies this slot:

1. **The scored application file (10 marks).** Motivation statement (5) + SEE/BLE score (5). This is *the only school-level input to the NAO* and it is worth **10 % of the Round-1 total**.
2. **The NEB Grade 9–12 Science curriculum** — specifically the physics needed for the IOAA syllabus's "General Notes" clause: *"Basic concepts in physics and mathematics at high school level are required… Standard solutions should not involve calculus, complex numbers or differential equations."*
3. **The Junior Category itself** (classes 8–10) — which is functionally a district/school-age entry tier and, per Sauryam Shrestha's IAO 2025 bronze, **does produce international medallists.**

### 2.4 🔑 The syllabus is **two-thirds physics, one-third astronomy**

Sections 1–5 of the Senior syllabus (**Mechanics, Heat & Thermodynamics, Nuclear Physics, Wave & Optics, Electricity & Magnetism**) are **pure NEB Physics** — and they are exactly the sections your Physics file already covers. Sections 6–11 (**The Sun, The Earth, The Solar System, The Stars, Universe, Space Exploration**) are the astronomy-specific content, and Section 12 is mathematics.

**Practical consequence for your site:** the Astronomy and Physics Olympiad pages should **cross-link**. A student who has completed the Physics roadmap has already covered roughly **40 % of the NAO Senior syllabus** — the five hardest, most physics-dependent sections. → **Gap A-5.**

Note also that the Senior syllabus includes three items that appear in **no other Nepali olympiad syllabus**: **Hand Measurement / Sky Navigation (Altitude/Azimuth)**, **Bayer Designation**, and the **HR Diagram**. These are cheap, high-yield, purely memorisable-and-practiseable topics. → **Gap A-6.**

**A.3 Practical Part** — *"consists of 2 sections: **observations** and **data analysis**. The theoretical part of the Syllabus provides the basis for all problems in the practical part."*

> **The observations section focuses on contestant's experience in:**
> **1.** naked-eye observations
> **2.** usage of sky maps and catalogues
> **3.** application of coordinate systems in the sky, **magnitude estimation, estimation of angular separation**
> **4.** usage of basic astronomical instruments — telescopes and various detectors for observations, *but enough instructions must be provided*. Observational objects may be from **real sources in the sky or imitated sources in the laboratory**. **Computer simulations may be used** in the problems but sufficient instructions must be provided.
>
> **The data analysis section focuses on the calculation and analysis of the astronomical data provided in the problems. Additional requirements:**
> **1.** Proper identification of error sources, **calculation of errors**, and estimation of their influence on the final results
> **2.** Proper use of graph papers with different scales, e.g. **polar and logarithmic papers**. **Transformation of the data to get a linear plot** and finding a **"Best Fit" line** approximately
> **3.** **Basic statistical analysis** of the observational data
> **4.** Knowledge of the most common experimental techniques for measuring physical quantities mentioned in Part A
